Example: Aurora Versicherung AG

A fictional mid-sized Swiss insurer replaces its in-house non-life policy system with a standard package, line of business by line of business. Every level of the method, every decision type and both Solution Blueprint profiles appear in it.

The scenario

Aurora's strategy for 2026–2030 demands faster product launches, 30 % lower run cost in non-life and straight-through quote-and-bind for brokers. The in-house policy system BEST-NL blocks all three: every product change needs a code release. Aurora decides to replace it with the standard package NovaPolicy (fictional vendor, SaaS in a Swiss region).

Two constraints shape the architecture:

  • The strategy rules out a big bang. Motor moves first, household and liability a year later.
  • Claims and collections have neither budget nor capacity to change before 2029. They must keep receiving policy data in the old format.

The answer is a Policy Adapter Layer (own development) that receives data from both policy systems, routes by line of business, publishes canonical events, and keeps generating the legacy feeds for claims and collections.

Four trails through the example

The example is easiest to understand by following one thread through the levels.

Trail 1: a principle cascades down

  1. The Enterprise Blueprint defines EP-02: systems integrate via the integration platform, no point-to-point coupling (chapter 4.2).
  2. The Domain Blueprint refines it into DPP-02: consumers outside the domain are shielded from policy system changes (chapter 4.2, column "Refines").
  3. The DP Blueprint lists both as inherited constraints (chapter 2) and derives its strategy from them: the adapter layer is the only exit for policy data (chapter 4).
  4. The Policy Adapter Layer's System Blueprint shows EP-02 as partly met: the legacy feeds to claims and collections are direct file transfers. This is registered as technical debt TD-PANL-002 and covered by ADR-ENT-001.

Trail 2: an exception from request to register

  1. Standard STD-LOG-01 requires security logs in the SIEM within 15 minutes. NovaPolicy delivers only daily files until vendor release 2028.1.
  2. Triage classifies the change as Deviation: the light SB-004 is written.
  3. The exception ADR-SYS-NOVA-002 records the compensating control, the expiry date 2028-06-30 and the path back to compliance.
  4. On return, the exception appears in chapter 11 of the NovaPolicy, DP and Enterprise Blueprints, and chapter 13 of NovaPolicy shows the vendor release that closes it.
Note

The example still shows the DP level deciding this exception. Under the current delegation rule, an exception of about 19 months on logs that contain personal data is decided by the owner of the standard, the CISO. See finding F1 in the findings.

Trail 3: a waiver changes a principle

  1. EP-04 version 1 demanded one central product platform for all lines of business.
  2. NovaPolicy's value rests on its integrated product engine. Splitting it off would destroy most of the benefit, permanently. An exception would be dishonest, because there is no path back.
  3. ADR-ENT-003 is a waiver with a deadline for changing the principle. EP-04 version 2 was published, the waiver closed.
  4. Every blueprint below now inherits EP-04 v2, visible in the NovaPolicy compliance table.

Trail 4: an initiative in plateaus

  1. The model extract defines plateaus PL-0 to PL-3 and work packages WP-1 to WP-5.
  2. Chapter 13 of the Enterprise, Domain and DP Blueprints shows the same plateaus at their level of detail: the DP Blueprint lists which systems are added or retired per plateau.
  3. SB-001 delivers PL-1. It introduces a temporary migration factory, takes the decision to migrate actively at a cutover weekend (ADR-SOL-001-01), and lists in its return checklist where each result goes.
  4. SB-002 for PL-2 will reuse SB-001; at that point "LoB migration" becomes an established domain pattern.

Enhancement: a customer portal

In November 2026, a new demand arrives: private customers want to see their policies online and buy simple products without an intermediary. This section shows how the existing target pictures are extended, following the enhancement steps of the method.

1 Triage: the demand has no strategic goal

The triage finds a change across three domains (customer & distribution, product & policy, technology platforms), personal data exposed to the internet, and a dependency on the core replacement. It also finds that none of the goals SG-1 to SG-4 covers a direct customer channel, and that online sales would compete with Aurora's brokers. The demand is escalated to the enterprise level.

4 What changes on return

SB-005 describes the delta for plateau PL-C1. Its return checklist lists what follows after implementation: a new DP Blueprint Customer Self-Service and System Blueprint Customer Portal, the Policy Adapter Layer's System Blueprint with read store and query API, the DP context of Policy Administration Non-Life, and the lifecycle of the new model objects from planned to target.

What the example taught about the method

Writing the example exposed gaps in the method, for example legacy systems without a fitting profile, or decisions taken during a Solution Blueprint that belong to a higher level from the start. They are collected with proposals in the findings, together with the decisions already taken.
