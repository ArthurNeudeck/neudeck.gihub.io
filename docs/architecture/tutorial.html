Tutorial: setting up target pictures

Eleven steps from the first sponsor conversation to a living set of blueprints. Each step says what to do, what you get, when you are done, and where to find it in the Aurora example.

Before you start

This tutorial assumes you know the method in outline. It is written for the architect who introduces it: a chief architect, the head of an enterprise architecture team, or in a small company the one architect who covers everything.

Start with a vertical slice, not with completeness

The most common failure is to start top-down and try to finish every Enterprise and Domain Blueprint before the first system is touched. The documents are outdated before anyone uses them. Instead, pick one real change that matters to the business and build a vertical slice through all levels it touches:

  • a lean Enterprise Blueprint (goals, capability heatmap, five to eight principles, domain map),
  • the one Domain Blueprint the change lives in,
  • the DP Blueprint and the System Blueprints of the affected systems,
  • the Solution Blueprint for the change itself.

Aurora did exactly this: the replacement of the non-life policy system drove the first version of every level. Other domains and DPs follow when their next significant change comes up.

How the steps are organised

Each step has a goal, the people involved, the actions, the outcome and a "done when" criterion. Notes for small and large organisations refer to the profiles on the scaling page. Decide your profile in step 1.

The steps

1Agree on purpose, sponsor and scale

Goal
A mandate: why the organisation wants connected target pictures, who sponsors them, and how many levels are needed.
Who
Chief architect, CIO or CTO as sponsor, one or two business leaders.
Outcome
One-page mandate; chosen size profile; the change that drives the vertical slice.
  1. Collect three to five concrete pains, for example "nobody knew the claims system depends on the policy feed" or "exceptions granted in 2019 are still open". They are your success measures.
  2. Choose the size profile (small, mid-sized, large) on the scaling page. It decides which levels you introduce now.
  3. Pick the driving change for the vertical slice: important, starting within the next months, touching at least two systems.
  4. Agree who owns the method itself (usually the chief architect) and where blueprints live (a Git repository, published to an intranet or wiki).

Done when the sponsor has confirmed the mandate and the driving change.

2Set up the repository and the minimum model

Goal
A model that blueprints can reference and checks can rely on.
Who
Enterprise architects, repository administrator, DP managers for their objects.
Outcome
Metamodel with ID conventions; systems, interfaces, business objects, capabilities, principles and standards of the slice modelled.
  1. Configure the ArchiMate repository with the attributes of the minimum modelling depth: aliases, domain and DP, profile, lifecycle, hosting and PII for systems; classification and system of record for business objects; owner function, anchor level and applicability rule for principles and standards.
  2. Fix ID conventions early and never reuse an ID: for example SYS-, IF-, BO-, CAP-, EP- (enterprise principle), STD-, PL- (plateau), WP- (work package).
  3. Model the systems of the slice and their direct neighbours, including aliases people actually use.
  4. Model standards as objects with an applicability rule ("applies when hosting = vendor SaaS"). This is what later lets the gate derive chapter 2 of a System Blueprint.
  5. Set up the Git repository with the template set and a folder per level.
Aurora

The model extract shows the minimum model as tables: 12 systems with aliases, 11 interfaces, 6 business objects with classification, 8 principles and 6 standards with applicability rules.

small A spreadsheet or YAML files in Git can stand in for the repository at first, as long as IDs are stable. large Agree the metamodel across all entities before modelling starts; import system inventories from the CMDB instead of typing them.

Done when every system of the slice has a complete set of minimum attributes.

3Write the Enterprise Blueprint

Goal
The strategic frame every other blueprint refers to.
Who
Chief architect; reviewed by the Architecture Board and the sponsor.
Template
enterprise-blueprint.md
  1. Chapter 1: translate the business strategy into three to six measurable strategic goals. Build the capability map (an industry reference model saves weeks) and colour it as a heatmap: where does the strategy demand change? Link every red capability to a goal.
  2. Chapter 2: list regulatory and group constraints as model references.
  3. Chapter 4: write the architecture strategy in a few bullets (buy before make, replacement approach, integration approach), then five to eight enterprise principles. Each principle needs a statement, rationale and implications, and an owner. Fewer, sharper principles are better: every principle will appear in every blueprint below.
  4. List the standards in chapter 4.3, generated from the model.
  5. Chapter 5: the domain map, with a domain architect per domain (see step 4).
  6. Chapters 9, 11, 13: start empty or with the first cross-domain decisions; roadmap plateaus come from step 9.
Aurora

The Enterprise Blueprint has four strategic goals, a heatmap with two red capabilities (product development and policy administration), six principles and three enterprise ADRs. That is enough.

Done when the Architecture Board has approved version 1 and each red capability is linked to a goal.

4Cut domains and digital products

Goal
A stable structure for accountability, budgets and decisions.
Who
Chief architect with business and IT leadership; domain architects once named.
Outcome
Domain map; DP list with DP Manager Business, DP Manager IT and solution architect; every system assigned to exactly one DP.
  1. Cut domains along business capabilities, not along the org chart or along systems. Five to ten domains are typical for a mid-sized company.
  2. Put shared platforms (integration, identity, logging, archive) into a technology platform domain. Using a platform through its standard offering then does not count as a cross-domain change.
  3. Within each domain, group systems into digital products that deliver one business outcome and can be planned and funded together. Name both DP managers and one solution architect per DP.
  4. Check the rules: each DP in exactly one domain, each system in exactly one DP.
Aurora

Five domains, eight DPs. Product & Policy holds two DPs (non-life and life policy administration); platforms such as integration and security services sit in Technology Platforms.

Done when the domain map is in the Enterprise Blueprint and every system of the slice has a DP.

5Write the Domain Blueprint

Goal
Translate the enterprise frame into one business area and name its reusable patterns.
Who
Domain architect; reviewed by another domain architect.
Template
domain-blueprint.md
  1. Chapter 2: list every enterprise principle and relevant standard and write what it means for this domain. This is where the cascade becomes concrete.
  2. Chapter 4: add domain principles only where they refine an enterprise principle (state which one). A domain principle that contradicts one needs a waiver.
  3. Chapter 5: the DPs of the domain with their owners, and the business objects with data classification.
  4. Chapter 8: patterns. Start with candidates and mark their status; a pattern becomes established when a solution is reused a second time.
Aurora

The Product & Policy blueprint refines EP-02 (integration platform) into DPP-02 "consumers outside the domain are shielded from policy system changes", which later justifies the Policy Adapter Layer.

Done when chapter 2 covers all enterprise principles and the gate finds no missing inheritance.

6Write DP and System Blueprints

Goal
Make ownership, systems and their compliance visible where the work happens.
Who
DP managers (accountable), solution architects and lead engineers (authors).
Templates
dp-blueprint.md, system-blueprint-buy.md, system-blueprint-make.md
  1. DP Blueprint: outcomes linked to strategic goals, ownership table (who is accountable for which model objects), context with all cross-DP interfaces, the systems with profile and lifecycle (target, legacy, phase-out, temporary), and the roadmap.
  2. Choose the System Blueprint profile per system: Buy for standard software and SaaS, Make for own development.
  3. Chapter 2 of each System Blueprint is a compliance table. For each principle and standard that applies by the system's attributes, state why it applies and whether it is fulfilled. Every partly or no needs an ADR (step 7).
  4. Prioritise: write System Blueprints first for target systems and systems under change. Stable systems can wait until their next change; they are already in the model.
Aurora

Compare the NovaPolicy (Buy) and Policy Adapter Layer (Make) blueprints: the first documents modules, configuration and extensions, the second components and runtime. NovaPolicy's chapter 2 shows a "no" for the logging standard, resolved by an exception.

Done when every system of the slice that is a target or under change has an approved System Blueprint and every non-compliance has an ADR.

7Set up decision rights

Goal
Everyone knows where a decision is taken and who may grant a deviation.
Who
Architecture Board, function owners (CISO, data protection, compliance), domain architects.
Template
adr-template.md
  1. Publish the placement rule: decisions at the lowest level that covers all parties.
  2. Assign an owner function and an anchor level to every principle and standard in the model.
  3. Adopt the default delegation matrix and ask each owner whether to tighten or loosen it for their objects.
  4. Introduce the three ADR types (decision, exception, waiver) and an exception register: chapter 11 of each blueprint, aggregated in the Enterprise Blueprint with expiry dates.
  5. Agree what happens when an exception expires: it is either closed (compliance reached), renewed by the same authority with a new rationale, or turned into a waiver.
Aurora

Three decision types at enterprise level: ADR-ENT-001 (decision), ADR-ENT-002 (exception: two systems of record during migration) and ADR-ENT-003 (waiver that led to principle EP-04 version 2).

Done when every principle and standard has an owner and the first exceptions are in the register with expiry dates.

8Introduce triage and Solution Blueprints

Goal
Every change gets exactly as much architecture as it needs, and its results flow back.
Who
Solution architects, product owners, delivery teams.
Template
solution-blueprint.md
  1. Add the architecture triage to the Definition of Ready. Pre-fill answers from the model: affected systems, interfaces, DPs, personal data.
  2. Map the result to the four change classes: Run, Deviation, Change, Initiative.
  3. For a Deviation, write a light Solution Blueprint: goal, triage result, the constraint not met, the ADR and the risk. For a Change, write a full one that describes only the delta.
  4. Make the return checklist part of the Definition of Done. Nothing is archived before ADRs, blueprints and the model are updated.
Aurora

SB-004 is a complete light Solution Blueprint on one page, already returned. SB-001 is a full one for the first plateau, with its return still open.

Done when the first change has gone through triage, Solution Blueprint and return.

9Plan initiatives in plateaus

Goal
Large programmes reach the target through stable, plannable intermediate states.
Who
Programme lead, domain and solution architects.
Outcome
Plateaus, gaps and work packages in the repository; chapter 13 of every affected blueprint.
  1. Describe the baseline (plateau 0) and the target, then the intermediate plateaus. A plateau is a state you could stay in for months if the programme stopped.
  2. For each plateau, model the gaps (what is added, changed, retired) and the work packages that close them.
  3. Model temporary systems and interfaces (migration tools, bridges) with lifecycle "temporary" and an end date, so they are retired, not forgotten.
  4. Plan one Solution Blueprint per plateau and generate the programme view as a report from the repository.
Aurora

Four plateaus from "all non-life on the old system" (PL-0) to "old system decommissioned" (PL-3), five work packages, one Solution Blueprint per plateau. See chapter 13 of the DP Blueprint.

Done when chapter 13 of the affected blueprints shows the same plateaus as the repository.

10Automate the quality gate and publishing

Goal
Consistency is checked by machines, so reviews can focus on content.
Who
An architect with engineering support; platform team for the pipeline.
Outcome
A pipeline that checks every commit, blocks on errors and publishes approved blueprints with current diagrams.
  1. Start with the cheapest deterministic rules: front matter complete, required chapters present, reviewer differs from responsible, referenced IDs exist in a model export.
  2. Add cascade rules: chapter 2 contains every applicable principle and standard; every partly or no has an ADR; exceptions have a future expiry and an allowed deciding level.
  3. Add publishing: replace diagram markers with the current repository export, render Markdown and publish to the intranet.
  4. Only then add AI checks against the model export, as hints and warnings. Promote an AI finding to an error only after a human confirms it.

small Run the rule list as a review checklist until automation pays off. large The gate is not optional: without it, the cascade breaks within a year.

Done when a pull request with a missing chapter 2 entry is blocked automatically.

11Keep the blueprints alive and expand

Goal
Blueprints stay true, and the method spreads to the rest of the organisation.
Who
All blueprint owners; the chief architect as method owner.
  1. Review each living blueprint at least yearly (last_reviewed in the front matter), and whenever a Solution Blueprint returns into it.
  2. Publish model quality figures in chapter 11 of the Enterprise Blueprint: share of systems with DP, complete interfaces, classified business objects.
  3. Run a monthly look at expiring exceptions and open waivers.
  4. Harvest patterns: when a Solution Blueprint is reused a second time, promote the solution to a domain pattern.
  5. Expand slice by slice: the next domain joins when its next significant change starts.

Done when never. The success measures from step 1 tell you whether it works.

Common pitfalls

  • Copying model content into text. System lists typed into a document are wrong within weeks. Reference IDs and generate lists.
  • Too many principles. Twenty enterprise principles produce twenty rows in every chapter 2 and nobody reads them. Keep five to eight.
  • Exceptions without expiry. An exception without date is a silent waiver. The gate should refuse it.
  • Solution Blueprints that never return. If the return is not in the Definition of Done, the living blueprints decay and the next change starts from wrong assumptions.
  • A review board for everything. Most changes are Run. Let the triage and the gate handle them; reserve boards for Change and Initiative.
