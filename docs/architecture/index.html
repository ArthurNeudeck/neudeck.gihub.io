Architecture Blueprints

A practical method for architecture target pictures on every level of an organisation: from enterprise strategy down to the individual system and the change that is under way right now. With templates, governance rules and a complete worked example.

Why target pictures on every level?

Most organisations have target pictures, but they rarely connect. The enterprise strategy lives in board slides, domain roadmaps in spreadsheets, and system documentation in a wiki that the teams write for themselves. When a project starts, nobody can say which principles apply, who decides a deviation, or which target picture the change has to update afterwards. Decisions get lost, exceptions never expire, and the target pictures drift away from reality.

This method connects the levels. Every level has a blueprint: a living document with the same structure, which inherits the principles of the level above, adds its own, and names the building blocks of the level below. Changes get a temporary blueprint that describes only the delta and is returned into the living blueprints before it is archived.

Six ideas the method rests on

  1. One skeleton for all levels. Every blueprint uses the 12 chapters of arc42 plus a 13th chapter for roadmap and transition. A profile per level says which chapters are required. Readers find the same content in the same place on every level.
  2. Model first. Systems, interfaces, business objects, capabilities, principles and standards are objects in an architecture repository (ArchiMate). Documents reference their IDs and never copy them. Diagrams come from the repository.
  3. Principles cascade. What a level defines as principles and patterns (chapters 4 and 8) appears as inherited constraints in chapter 2 of every blueprint below. Lower levels may refine a principle, never contradict it without a waiver.
  4. Decisions where they belong. A decision is taken at the lowest level that covers all parties. Deviations from a principle or standard are decided by its owner and recorded as exception (temporary) or waiver (permanent).
  5. Changes are temporary. A Solution Blueprint describes only the delta. Before it is archived, its decisions and changes are returned into the living blueprints and the model.
  6. A quality gate, not a review board for everything. Deterministic checks run on every commit and block only on clear rule violations; AI findings advise and escalate only after a human confirms them.

A complete worked example

Aurora Versicherung AG, a fictional mid-sized Swiss insurer, replaces its in-house non-life policy system with a standard package, line of business by line of business, while claims and collections stay on their legacy systems. The example contains a blueprint on every level, nine decisions including exceptions and a waiver, and both Solution Blueprint profiles. Every document can be read right here on the site.

Foundations

The method invents as little as possible. It combines established standards and adds only the rules that connect them.
