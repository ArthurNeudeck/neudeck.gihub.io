The method

The complete reference: which artefacts exist on which level, how they are structured, how principles and decisions flow between them, and how changes find their way back into the target pictures.

1 Levels and artefacts

The method distinguishes four living levels and two temporary ones. Living blueprints are maintained continuously and always describe the current target picture. Temporary artefacts exist only while a change or an initiative is running.

LevelArtefactLifetimeBuilding blocks described (chapter 5)
EnterpriseEnterprise Blueprintlivingdomains
DomainDomain Blueprint, including principles, patterns and ADRslivingdigital products
Digital Product (DP)DP Blueprint: arc42, roadmap, target picturelivingsystems
SystemSystem Blueprint, profile Buy or Makelivingmodules and configuration (Buy) or components (Make)
ChangeSolution Blueprint, profile full or lighttemporary, archived after returnonly the delta
InitiativeProgramme view in the repository: plateaus, gaps, work packagestemporary, spans levels–

Why a digital product level?

Domains are too large to plan budgets and accountability for, systems are too small. A digital product groups the systems that together deliver a business outcome, and gives them a pair of accountable owners: a DP Manager Business and a DP Manager IT. Two rules keep the structure clean:

  • Every DP belongs to exactly one domain, and every system to exactly one DP.
  • Interaction across DPs is treated like interaction across domains: it raises the change class (see change process).

Shared platforms such as integration, identity, logging or archive are DPs of their own, typically in a technology platform domain.

2 One skeleton for all levels

Every blueprint follows the arc42 chapters, extended by chapter 13 for roadmap and transition. The meaning of each chapter stays the same on every level; only the subject changes. This is what makes the cascade work: an architect always knows where to look.

1 Introduction and goalsbusiness goals; on enterprise level the strategy and a capability heatmap
2 Constraintsprinciples and standards inherited from the levels above, with the local interpretation
3 Contextthe boundary of the described object and its neighbours

5 Building blocksthe building blocks of the next lower level: domains, DPs, systems, components

Which chapters are required for which profile is defined in the chapter matrix of the template set. As a rule of thumb: chapters 1, 2, 9 and 11 are required everywhere; runtime and deployment matter on system level; roadmap matters on enterprise, domain and DP level.

Buy and Make profiles

A purchased system and a self-built one need different documentation. The Buy profile treats the vendor's internals as a black box and documents what the organisation owns: licensed modules in use, configuration, extension points and their upgrade risk, hosting and release cycle. The Make profile documents components, runtime and deployment in classic arc42 depth.

3 Model first

Text and model have separate masters. Markdown in Git is the master for text and reasoning; the architecture repository: a modelling tool for ArchiMate, the standard language for enterprise architecture models. The example uses ADOIT by BOC Group; any ArchiMate repository works is the master for every model element and every diagram.

  • Everything that is checked or linked is a repository object, including principles and standards. Documents reference repository IDs and never duplicate their content.
  • Diagrams live in the repository and are embedded with a marker such as <!-- adoit-view: VIEW-ID -->; the publishing pipeline replaces it with the current export.
  • No manual ID linking. Relations are maintained in the repository, not typed into documents twice.

Minimum modelling depth

The model has to be good enough for automated checks, not complete. The minimum is:

ObjectRequired attributes and relationsEnables
SystemID, name, aliases, domain and DP, profile (buy/make), lifecycle, hosting, PII flagfinding systems in text by alias, deriving applicable standards
Interfacesource, target, type, business objects, lifecyclecontext views, detecting cross-DP changes
Business objectdata classification, PII, system of recordprivacy and residency checks
Capabilityrelation to systems and strategic goals, heatheatmaps, impact of changes
Principle, standardowner function, anchor level, version, applicability rulecascade check, exception authority

Systems carry aliases because people write "Nova" or "the new policy system" instead of an ID. With aliases, an automated check can still connect text to model.

Notation

All diagrams are ArchiMate views. The method does not add a second notation such as C4, but applies C4's zoom discipline inside ArchiMate viewpoints: each level shows its own building blocks and their direct neighbours, nothing more. UML sequence diagrams are allowed as an exception for runtime views. BPMN is used for business processes where a domain needs them.

4 The principle cascade

Principles and patterns flow downwards. What a level defines in chapters 4 and 8 appears in chapter 2 of every blueprint below it, together with the local interpretation. A lower level may refine a principle (make it more specific), but never contradict it without a waiver.

On system level, chapter 2 turns into a compliance table: for every applicable principle and standard, the blueprint states why it applies (derived from system attributes) and whether it is fulfilled yes, partly or no. Anything other than yes needs an ADR. This is the place where target picture and reality meet.

5 Decisions, exceptions and waivers

All decisions are Architecture Decision Records stored with the living blueprint that owns them. There are three types:

TypeUseRequired extra fields
decisionan architecture choice within the rules–
exceptiona temporary deviation from a principle or standardowner, deciding level, compensating control, expiry date, technical debt ticket, path back to compliance
waivera permanent deviation; it forces a change of the principlerequired change of the principle, owner, deadline; stays open until the principle is changed

Every ADR ends with a rationale for the level below: a plain-language explanation that teams who have to live with the decision can understand.

Where a decision is taken

A decision is taken at the lowest level that covers all parties: system, then DP, then domain, then enterprise. A decision inside one system stays in its System Blueprint; one that changes several systems of a DP goes to the DP Blueprint; one that forces other domains to act goes to the Enterprise Blueprint.

Who decides deviations

A deviation is decided by the owner of the principle or standard that is not met. Every principle and standard has an owner function (for example the CISO for a security standard, the Architecture Board for an enterprise principle) and an anchor level that places it in the cascade. Both are repository attributes.

To keep owners from becoming a bottleneck, exceptions are delegated by a default matrix:

ExceptionDecided by
up to 6 months, compensating control, no data above "internal"DP level
up to 12 months, compensating controlDomain level
everything else, and every waiverowner of the principle or standard

Owners may tighten or loosen the matrix for their object, for example "data residency: always the owner". Every criterion can be checked automatically: the duration from date and expiry, the compensating control as a required field, the data classification from the model.

6 The change process

Every demand passes an architecture triage at Definition of Ready. The triage is a short questionnaire (about 14 questions), pre-filled from the repository wherever possible: which systems, interfaces, DPs and business objects are affected, whether personal data is involved, whether principles or standards are not met. Its result is a change class, and the class decides the artefact.

Return before archive

A Solution Blueprint describes only the delta to the living blueprints. When the change is implemented, a return checklist moves everything that remains valid to where it belongs:

  • ADRs move to the lowest living blueprint that covers all parties.
  • System, DP and Domain Blueprints are updated.
  • The model is updated: new interfaces, retired temporary systems, plateau status.
  • Exceptions and technical debt are registered with owner and expiry date.
  • Reuse is checked: a solution reused a second time becomes a domain pattern.

Only then is the Solution Blueprint archived. Reusing a solution creates a new Solution Blueprint that references its predecessor, never an edit of the old one.

Initiatives and the programme view

Large programmes move through plateaus: stable intermediate states such as "motor live on the new system". Plateaus, gaps and work packages are modelled in the repository (TOGAF vocabulary) and appear in chapter 13 of every affected blueprint. Each plateau is delivered by one Solution Blueprint. There is no hand-written programme document: the programme view is a report generated from the repository.

7 The quality gate

Blueprints live in Git. Every commit runs checks; merge and publication are blocked only by errors.

SeverityEffectSource
HintadvisoryAI
Warningmust be acknowledged in the pull requestAI or rules
Errorblocks merge and publicationdeterministic rules; AI findings only after a human confirms them

Typical deterministic rules: required chapters of the profile present; front matter complete; reviewer differs from responsible; every referenced ID exists in the model; chapter 2 lists all principles and standards that apply by system attributes; every partly or no has an ADR; every exception has an expiry date in the future and a deciding level allowed by the delegation matrix.

AI checks compare the text with an export of the model, never with diagram images. They find, for example, a system mentioned by alias but missing from the context chapter, or a claim that contradicts a principle. An error is overridden only by the owner of the violated rule, via ADR.

8 Enhancing the target pictures

Target pictures are never finished. New demands arrive that the current blueprints do not cover: a new channel, a new product, a regulation. The method handles them with the same building blocks as any other change, but one rule matters more than usual: each decision is recorded at its own level as soon as it is taken, while everything that depends on implementation returns later.

flowchart TB D[New demand] --> T{Triage
incl. strategic fit} T -->|"no strategic goal covers it"| E["Enterprise level:
new or changed goal + ADR"] T -->|"goal exists"| P E --> P["Place each decision at its level:
enterprise, domain, DP, system"] P --> M["Model new objects in the repository
with lifecycle 'planned'"] M --> R["Plan plateaus, incl. dependencies
on other programmes"] R --> S["Solution Blueprint per plateau
(the delta)"] S --> I[Implementation] I --> RET["Return: remaining blueprints updated,
'planned' becomes 'target'"]
  1. Triage, including strategic fit. Which strategic goal does the demand serve? If none does, the demand is not rejected but escalated: the enterprise level decides whether the strategy changes. (Making this a fixed triage question is open point F12 in the findings.)
  2. Strategy first. A new or changed goal goes into chapter 1 of the Enterprise Blueprint, the reasoning into an enterprise ADR, and the capability heatmap is recoloured.
  3. Decisions at their level, immediately. Every decision the demand forces is placed by the usual rule (lowest level covering all parties; deviations by the owner of the rule) and stored in that level's blueprint right away. The Solution Blueprint lists these ADRs but does not own them.
  4. Model the planned state. New systems, interfaces and attributes are created in the repository with lifecycle planned. That lets roadmaps (chapter 13) and context views show the coming target picture before anything is built.
  5. Plateaus and dependencies. The enhancement gets its own plateaus. If they depend on another programme's plateaus, the dependency is shown in every affected roadmap.
  6. Solution Blueprint for the delta, one per plateau, with the return checklist naming every living blueprint that still has to change.
  7. Return. After implementation, DP and System Blueprints are written or updated, the remaining ADRs move to their owners, and planned becomes target in the model.

Worked example

Aurora adds a customer portal: a new goal SG-5 and channel decision ADR-ENT-004 at enterprise level, a customer identity platform (ADR-ENT-005), a policy query API decided in the policy domain (ADR-DOM-002), and Solution Blueprint SB-005 for the delta.
