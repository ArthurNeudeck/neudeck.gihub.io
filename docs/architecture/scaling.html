Sizing for small and large companies

The method has six artefact types and four living levels. A 40-person company needs fewer of them, a group with several legal entities needs more. This page shows what to keep, what to collapse and what to add.

The scaling rule

Levels exist to place decisions and accountability. A level is worth introducing only if someone is accountable for it and decisions are regularly taken at it. If nobody would own a Domain Blueprint, do not write one: let the level above cover it. If two business units decide independently about the same kind of question, they need their own level.

Whatever the size, three things never go away:

  • the same chapter skeleton on every level you keep,
  • stable IDs for systems, principles and standards,
  • ADRs with owner and expiry for every deviation.

Small organisations

In a small company, the same few people sit at every level. Separate documents per level would only repeat each other. Collapse instead:

  • One Enterprise Blueprint does the work of three levels. Chapter 4 contains the enterprise principles, chapter 5 lists the digital products with their systems and owners, chapter 8 the patterns. Add a domain section only if you really have distinct business areas with their own rules.
  • System Blueprints for what matters. The core system, every own development and every system with personal data. A SaaS tool with no integration is a row in the model, nothing more.
  • Lightweight model. A table with stable IDs and the minimum attributes is enough to start. Move to a real repository when you have more than about 40 systems or need generated views.
  • Decision rights stay explicit. Even if the CTO decides almost everything, write the ADRs. The exception register is the cheapest risk management you can get.
  • Triage in five questions: Which systems change? Does an interface to another product change? Is personal data affected? Is a principle or standard not met? Is it part of a larger programme?
Minimal set for a small company

One Enterprise Blueprint, System Blueprints for core systems, the ADR template, the light Solution Blueprint, and a pull request checklist. That is five templates out of eight.

Mid-sized organisations

The method is designed for this size; the Aurora example is mid-sized. Use all levels and both Solution Blueprint profiles. Two things decide whether it works:

  • Accountable DP managers. Without named business and IT owners per DP, model objects are not maintained and the DP Blueprints decay.
  • Automated deterministic checks before the second domain joins. Manual review does not keep the cascade consistent beyond one domain.

Large organisations

Large organisations need more levels, not bigger documents. The skeleton stays the same; the hierarchy grows.

Adding levels

  • Group level above Enterprise. A Group Blueprint uses the enterprise profile and holds group principles and standards, typically regulatory, security and shared platforms. Each legal entity or country keeps its own Enterprise Blueprint and inherits the group principles in chapter 2.
  • Sub-domains. If a domain has more than about ten DPs, split it. A sub-domain uses the domain profile; its parent domain's chapter 5 lists sub-domains instead of DPs.
  • Shared domains across entities. A domain that serves several entities (for example a group-wide finance platform) is owned at group level and appears in each entity's Enterprise Blueprint as a building block.

Federated governance

  • Owner functions exist at several levels. A group CISO owns group security standards; an entity CISO may own an entity-specific refinement. The owner of the violated standard decides, as everywhere in the method.
  • Delegation matrix per entity. Group owners delegate to entity level for exceptions within defined bounds; entities delegate further to domains and DPs.
  • One metamodel. Agree attributes and ID conventions across the group before modelling. Prefix IDs per entity if needed (CH-SYS-, DE-SYS-).
  • Generated views only. Programme views, exception registers and model quality figures must be reports from the repository. Hand-maintained overviews do not survive at this scale.
